02 — Frame, fork and handlebar

The China group

The carbon frame, the Grit fork and the Smoothie handlebar are 39.5% of input cost. All three are made in China, and they are where Lauf's own design sits.

The group pays 39.0% today. It reached 171.5% between 10 April and 13 May 2025.

The two notches in 2021 and 2024 are a Section 301 exclusion for carbon frames expiring, being reinstated, and expiring again.

Why these three

Lauf began as a fork company. The leaf-spring fork is the product the business was built on, and the Grit on this bike is its third generation. No one else makes one.

The Smoothie handlebar is the other part Lauf designs itself — glass fibre laid into the carbon, which roughly doubles the compliance of a normal carbon bar.

And the frame is what defines any bike brand: its own mould, its own tooling, its own geometry.

These are the parts Lauf cannot switch away from. Changing supplier means new moulds and new tooling, not a new line on a spec sheet — and all three are made in China.

03 — Everything else

The other components

Sixteen parts — wheels, drivetrain, brakes, saddle, stem, tyres — are 60.5% of input cost. Almost all are Taiwanese; the chain is from the EU. All are catalogue parts.

The group pays 10.4%. Its high was 37.9%, for one day, on 9 April 2025. The chain moved to 50% in April 2026 under Section 232 steel.

Who makes them

Eight of the sixteen are SRAM: the Rival AXS shifters and derailleur, the cassette, crankset, chain, bottom bracket, rotors and the AXS electronics. Together that is 33.4% of the bike's input cost — more than half of this group.

That is less a Lauf decision than the state of the market. Shimano and SRAM supply almost all shifting on mid- and upper-tier bikes, usually put at 95–98%; one industry count has them at 99% of mountain bikes with shifting components.

The rest — wheels, tyres, saddle, seatpost, stem, headset — are catalogue parts from e*thirteen, Goodyear, Selle Italia and FSA. Any of them can be specified differently next season without touching the design of the bike.

Method

Rates come from a daily model of 14 HTS provisions across 5 origins, 20 January 2017 to 10 August 2026. The blended series is the value-share-weighted mean of all 18 components and matches the workbook's constructed-bike series. Each group series is the weighted mean within that group, so it reads as the rate that group pays rather than its contribution to the total.

China group: carbon frame (8714.91.3000), carbon fork (8714.91.9000), carbon handlebar (8714.99.8000) — 39.5% of input cost. Everything else: fifteen Taiwanese items plus the EU chain — 60.5%.

The SRAM share is the eight line items supplied by SRAM on this build — shifters and brakes, rear derailleur, cassette, crankset, chain, bottom bracket, rotors and AXS electronics — summed by value share. Component supplier attribution is from the published Club spec, not the workbook. The 95–98% figure for Shimano and SRAM's combined share of mid- and upper-tier shifting is a widely quoted industry estimate; the 99% mountain-bike figure is from Cycling Industry News.

Assumptions

Ordinary consumption entries, no transaction-specific relief. The carbon-frame Section 301 exclusion is modelled as qualifying; it applies only below a stated customs value per frame. The chain takes the full 50% Section 232 derivative rate from 6 April 2026, which assumes no U.S. steel content exception. Stem and through-axle classifications are as supplied.
